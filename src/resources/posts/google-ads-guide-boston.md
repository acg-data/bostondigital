If you run a local Boston business and you're not running Google Ads, you're leaving customers on the table — they're literally typing your offering into Google right now. This guide is what we'd tell a friend who asked "how do I start with Google Ads?"

## Step 1: Pick the right campaign type

For local service businesses, you'll use one of three:

- **Search campaigns** — when someone Googles "plumber near me," your ad shows. Highest intent, highest ROI starting point.
- **Local Service Ads (LSAs)** — separate from Search, you pay per lead, you get the "Google Guaranteed" badge. Best for trade services.
- **Performance Max** — Google's AI campaign type. Powerful but opaque. Don't start here.

**Our advice:** Start with Search. Add LSAs after 30 days if you qualify. Test Performance Max only after you have 6+ months of clean conversion data.

## Step 2: Structure your account around intent, not products

Bad structure (most agencies do this):

```
Campaign: All Services
  Ad Group: Bathroom
  Ad Group: Kitchen
  Ad Group: Basement
```

Good structure:

```
Campaign: Bathroom Remodel - High Intent
  Ad Group: "bathroom remodel near me"
  Ad Group: "bathroom contractor Boston"
Campaign: Bathroom Remodel - Research Phase
  Ad Group: "bathroom remodel cost"
  Ad Group: "bathroom remodel ideas"
```

Separating high-intent from research-phase queries lets you bid differently, write different ads, and route to different landing pages. CPA on the high-intent campaign will be 3-5x better than the research one.

## Step 3: Write ads that prequalify

Your ad isn't trying to get every click — it's trying to get the right clicks. Bake qualifying language into the ad:

- Price: "From $5,000" filters out budget shoppers
- Geography: "Serving Boston, Cambridge, Newton" filters out distant searchers
- Service area: "Residential only" or "Commercial only"
- Differentiator: "Family-owned 20+ years" — speaks to people who care

Each qualifier in the ad headline cuts wasted clicks. Lower CTR, but much higher conversion rate per click.

## Step 4: Negative keywords from day one

The single biggest source of waste in Google Ads is paying for queries that don't match your offering. Before you launch, add these universal negatives:

- "free"
- "DIY"
- "how to"
- "jobs" / "career" / "hiring"
- "wikipedia"
- competitor brand names you don't want to bid against
- Cities/regions you don't serve

Then check your Search Terms report weekly for the first 90 days and add more.

## Step 5: Set up conversion tracking BEFORE you launch

Launch with one or more of these tracking:

- **Phone call tracking** — use CallRail or Google's own call tracking
- **Form submissions** — fire a conversion when someone hits your thank-you page
- **Lead value** — assign an estimated value per lead (e.g., $200) so Google can optimize

No conversion tracking = no optimization signal = wasted budget. Don't launch without it.

## Step 6: Budget realistically

Most local Boston businesses need at least **$2,000/month** in media spend to gather meaningful data within 30 days. Below that, you're starving Google's algorithm.

Math: typical local service CPC is $8-$25. At $2,000/month, that's 80-250 clicks. At a 5% conversion rate, you get 4-12 leads/month — enough to start optimizing.

If you can only spend $500/month, run **Local Service Ads** instead. You pay per lead, not per click, and the volume is more predictable.

## Step 7: Optimize weekly

After launch, here's the rhythm:

- **Daily (first 2 weeks):** Watch for runaway spend, broken links, no impressions
- **Weekly:** Review Search Terms, add negatives, pause low-performers
- **Monthly:** Refresh ads, adjust bids, expand winning keywords

Most accounts stabilize at month 3. Month 1 is learning. Month 2 is optimizing. Month 3 is scaling.

## What this looks like in practice

A Boston home services client started with:

- Budget: $3,500/month
- CPA at launch: $215
- Lead volume: 16/month

After 90 days of the framework above:

- Budget: $3,500/month (same)
- CPA: $98 (54% reduction)
- Lead volume: 36/month (125% increase)

Same money. 2.25× the leads. That's what disciplined Google Ads management looks like.
