If you run a local service business, you've probably been told "you need to be on social media" — without a clear answer for which platforms, how often, or what to post. Most of that advice is wrong for your business model. Here's what actually works.

## Start with the question: where do your customers research you?

Before posting on any platform, ask the simpler question: where do your customers find you when they're researching the decision to buy from you?

For local service businesses, the answer is almost always:

1. **Google search** (#1 by a mile)
2. **Google Business Profile / Maps reviews**
3. **Facebook** (especially for over-35 demographics)
4. **Instagram** (especially for visual services — landscaping, med spa, dental, salons)
5. **TikTok / Reels** (the new under-30 search engine)

Notice what's not on the list: LinkedIn (B2B only), Twitter/X (largely irrelevant for local consumer services), Pinterest (only relevant for visual aspirational categories).

## Platform-by-platform recommendations

### Facebook: still the #1 ROI platform for local services

For service businesses targeting 35+ customers, Facebook is still the highest-leverage social platform — both organic and paid. The targeting is unmatched (neighborhood-level, life event, household income), and your customer base is already there.

**What to post:**
- Customer photos (before/after for visual services)
- Job site updates / behind-the-scenes
- Quick how-to videos (60-90 seconds)
- Customer reviews/testimonials with photos
- Hyper-local community involvement (sponsored youth sports, fundraisers)

**Frequency:** 3-5 posts per week minimum. Stories daily.

### Instagram: the showcase platform

If your service produces visible results (med spa, dental, landscaping, home improvement, salon), Instagram is non-negotiable. It's your portfolio.

**What works:**
- Before/after reels (15-30 seconds)
- Process videos (treatment day, install day, etc.)
- Educational carousels (10-slide explainers about your service)
- Team intros (humanize the business)

**What doesn't:**
- Stock photos with text overlay
- Pure promotional content with no story
- Posting once a week and expecting growth

**Frequency:** 4-6 posts per week, mix of reels (60%) and photo posts (40%). Stories daily.

### TikTok: rising fast, especially for service businesses

TikTok is the new local search engine for under-30 audiences. "best Boston med spa" / "Boston haircut review" / "Cambridge plumber" are real searches happening on TikTok daily.

**What works:**
- Behind-the-scenes raw footage (not polished)
- Voiceover explainers ("here's why most people overpay for X")
- Trends with your industry spin
- Talking-head videos with strong hooks

**Threshold to bother:** if your average customer is under 35, you should be on TikTok. If they're 50+, skip it for now.

### LinkedIn: only if you're B2B

If you sell to other businesses (B2B services, accountants, consultants, agencies), LinkedIn matters. Otherwise, ignore it.

### Pinterest: niche but powerful when it fits

For wedding services, home design/renovation, landscaping, and visual aspirational categories — Pinterest can drive surprising traffic for low effort. Skip otherwise.

## The 70/20/10 content rule

Mix your content roughly:

- **70% value** — educational, helpful, entertaining
- **20% community** — UGC, customer features, behind-the-scenes
- **10% promotional** — offers, services, calls to book

Most local businesses invert this and post 70% promotional content. That's why their engagement dies.

## Realistic benchmarks for local service businesses

What good looks like in 2026:

| Platform | Engagement rate | Reach growth | Lead gen |
|---|---|---|---|
| Facebook | 1-3% | 5-10% / month | 2-5 leads / 1000 followers / month |
| Instagram | 1-3% | 8-15% / month | 1-3 leads / 1000 followers / month |
| TikTok | 5-10% | 20-50% / month (early) | Hard to attribute directly |

If you're way below these, the issue is usually content quality, not posting frequency.

## The painful truth

Organic social media for local service businesses is a **brand-building** play, not a direct lead-gen play. The leads you'll attribute directly to social will be small (5-15% of total marketing leads). But social shapes how customers feel about you when they're ready to buy — and reviews + social proof are why your other channels (search, referrals) convert better.

Don't measure social in isolation. Measure it as the trust layer that makes everything else work.
