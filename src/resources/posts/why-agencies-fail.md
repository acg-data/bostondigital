Average agency-client tenure is about 9 months. That's a damning stat. Either agencies aren't delivering results, clients aren't getting what they expected, or both. Having worked at three agencies before starting this one, I've seen the pattern up close — and built our model to avoid it.

## Reason 1: Bait-and-switch staffing

You sell the deal with senior strategists. Then the work gets handed to a junior account manager who's also juggling 14 other accounts. The senior team appears only at QBRs to put out fires.

**How we operate differently:** The strategist who sells you is the strategist who runs your account. We cap our team at clients per strategist, not clients per agency. If we can't service you with our senior team, we say no to the deal.

## Reason 2: Bundled retainers with hidden waste

Most agencies sell "$5,000/month" packages that include 10 things, three of which actually move the needle. The other seven exist to justify the price.

**How we operate differently:** We scope every engagement to specific outcomes. If SEO is what moves your needle, we're not also charging for "monthly newsletter" or "podcast strategy" you don't need. Smaller engagements that compound — not bloated retainers.

## Reason 3: Vanity reporting

Monthly reports full of impressions, reach, and engagement — none of which pay your bills. Meanwhile, the agency avoids reporting on the metric that actually matters: revenue per dollar spent.

**How we operate differently:** Every monthly report leads with three numbers: leads generated, cost per lead, and revenue attributed. Everything else is supporting context. If those three numbers aren't trending the right direction, that's the conversation we have first.

## Reason 4: No skin in the game

Most agencies get paid the same whether you grow 10% or 100%. There's no shared incentive to push for outsized results.

**How we operate differently:** For long-term clients, we offer performance-based components — a base retainer plus a bonus tied to growth above a baseline. Not appropriate for every engagement, but where it fits, it aligns everyone.

## Reason 5: Slow communication

Need to change something? You email your account manager, who pings the specialist, who maybe gets to it next week. By the time changes happen, the moment is gone.

**How we operate differently:** We use a shared Slack channel with every client. Direct access to your strategist. Changes deploy in days, not weeks. Marketing moves at the speed of the market — your agency should too.

## Reason 6: Treating clients as transactions

The "land and expand" mindset: get the deal, deliver minimum viable work, upsell more services. Eventually the client realizes and churns.

**How we operate differently:** We over-deliver in month one. We make ourselves valuable enough that you don't want to leave. We've kept clients for 5+ years not because of contract handcuffs but because the partnership keeps working.

## What this looks like for you

If you're evaluating agencies, here are the questions worth asking:

1. **Who specifically will work on my account day-to-day?** (Get names. Verify their LinkedIn.)
2. **Show me a real monthly report from a comparable client** (redacted is fine — you want to see the format and metrics they prioritize)
3. **What's your average client tenure?** (Under 12 months is a red flag)
4. **What happens if we hit our growth target early?** (Are you incentivized to keep growing, or just maintain?)
5. **How do you handle communication when things change?** (Look for direct access, not gatekeepers)

The agencies worth working with will give straight answers to all five. The agencies that won't make eye contact... that's your answer.
